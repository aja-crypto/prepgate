***

***Q1) “Should I take a drop for GATE CSE 2027?”***

It honestly depends on your financial situation and your risk appetite. Sit down, think calmly, list the pros and cons, and then decide. Don’t take this decision emotionally.

Case 1: Your family depends on you financially

In that case, it’s safer to get a job and prepare alongside. Taking a full drop is a big risk.

Over the last few days, I spoke to many people who prepared sincerely (at least from what I could see), but still didn’t get the score they wanted. Effort doesn’t always guarantee results. Be practical.

Case 2: You’re in 3rd year or final year, no financial pressure, and scored 40+ in GATE CSE 2026

Then yes, you can consider preparing for one more year with full dedication — especially if your goal is a top institute or PSU.

If you’re serious, GATE should be the first thing on your mind when you wake up and the last thing before you sleep.

If you’re in final year, try for placements too. Even a low CTC job is fine. Having stability while preparing is not a bad option.

Case 3: You already took a drop for GATE CSE 2026

This is tricky.

Personally, I’d suggest either joining the best college you’re getting right now, or taking up a job and preparing alongside. A second drop needs very strong justification.

***

***Q2) “Which resources should I follow?”***

If you’re in college, start early. Don’t wait.

* **GoClasses** – They teach concepts in depth. It takes time, but it’s solid.
* **Amit Khurana (paid lectures)** – Especially helpful for Aptitude, DSA, TOC, and Discrete Mathematics. I personally benefited from his lectures.
* **Offline coaching** – If finances allow, a structured environment really helps.

I enrolled in ACE Academy’s offline program in Hyderabad (PM3 batch) based on a friend’s recommendation.

Bonus tip -

If you don’t understand something from one source, switch. Don’t get stuck. Be flexible.  
You can read selective portions from standard textbooks if needed — but it’s not compulsory.

PYQs -

Do PYQs along with syllabus completion. Don’t wait until the end of the subject.  
Do at least 3 iterations of PYQs for every subject.

What I followed -

* ACE Academy – All subjects (offline)
* GoClasses – Linear Algebra, Probability, Undecidability, Code Optimization
* Amit Khurana – Aptitude, DSA, TOC, Discrete Mathematics

***

***Q3) “How many months are required for a good rank?”***

Your rank depends on the 3 hours in the exam hall. Not on how long you prepared.

If your basics are weak, it may take more than a year.  
If your fundamentals are strong, 3–4 months of focused preparation can also work.

Don’t compare your timeline with others.

I had basic knowledge of most subjects before starting serious prep, and it still took me almost a year.

***

***Q4) “How many mocks should I give?”***

If analyzed properly, 20–25 full-length mocks are enough.

From what I’ve seen, GoClasses and Gate@Zeal have good quality mocks. GoClasses also provides free All India Mock Tests from December — definitely attempt those.

Don’t get discouraged by low scores initially. Analyze properly and improve.

If you want more practice, you can also try MadeEasy and ACE mocks.

Remember: analysis matters more than the number of mocks.

***

***Q5) “Daily schedule? How many hours?”***

I never counted hours.

During offline coaching:

* Classes from 7 AM to 12:30 PM
* Lunch + short nap
* Study hall in the evening to revise the day’s topics
* Made compressed notes simultaneously (saved a lot of time later)

After returning home, I naturally followed something like Pomodoro — focused sessions with short breaks.

Once you start full-length mocks, maintain an error log. It helps you see your repeating mistakes.

If GATE CSE 2027 is your priority, give it your full commitment.

Also:

* 30-minute walk daily
* Portion control
* Proper sleep

Health matters more than you think.

***

***Q6) “How do I score XX marks?”***

Don’t aim for XX.

Aim for 100.

Study like you need 100. If you aim high, you’ll push harder. That’s how 70–90 happens.

I used to visualize my future after getting a good rank almost every day. Call it manifestation or whatever you want, it helped me stay consistent.

At the end of the day, consistency beats bursts of motivation.

If you’re serious, treat it seriously. That’s it.

Also, learn as if you're learning the ABC of Computer Science. Assume you're starting from zero. It really helps.
